Drove up to Prashar Lake last month with two friends from college. The road from Mandi is the kind that makes you question every decision that brought you to that car. Tight switchbacks, no railing in places, the driver named Sushil who has done this drive 400 times saying "no worries sir" while overtaking on a curve.

We made it.

For people who haven't been: Prashar Lake sits at around 2,700 metres in the Dhauladhar range, with a small wooden temple on its bank that's apparently been there for more than a thousand years. The lake itself is unusually round and unusually still, and there's a small floating island in the middle of it that supposedly moves on its own. I have never seen it move. Locals will tell you it does. Believe what you want.

The thing about Prashar is the weather. You can leave Mandi at 30 degrees and arrive at 8 degrees, with mist rolling in from a direction that didn't exist when you got out of the car. The temperature drops another five after the sun goes down. We were in t-shirts at the trailhead and three layers each by dinner.

We stayed in one of the dhabas that put up tents on the meadow behind the lake. Rajma, rice, watery dal, the best meal of the week. The owner played FM Mandi on a cracked phone speaker while his son did homework in a notebook propped against a kerosene lantern. I asked the kid what subject. He said maths. I said "kaisi jaa rahi hai." He said "boring." Universal.

The walk around the lake at dawn is the actual thing. The mist comes off the water in slow sheets. The temple sits on the far side, dark wood against the grey, and the silence is so complete you start hearing your own ears. We didn't talk for about twenty minutes, which for three friends from engineering college is essentially a meditation retreat.

On the way back to Mandi we hit rain. Then sun. Then rain again. Then sun. The mountains do this. I forget every time. The drive that took five hours up took four going down because Sushil was, in his words, "experienced now." I chose not to ask experienced in what.

If you have a long weekend and a willingness to be cold at altitude, do this trip. The temple is over a thousand years old. The lake is small and weird and beautiful. The night sky, if it's clear, is the kind that makes you understand why people built temples next to lakes a thousand years ago.
