I used to think finishing a book was a moral obligation. If I started it, I had to finish it. The deal I'd made with myself around age sixteen, watching my dad read the same paperback for three months on weekends.

The deal was wrong. It cost me years of reading.

What kept happening: I'd start a book that didn't grab me. Push through the first fifty pages. Still wouldn't grab me. Put it on the bedside table to "come back to later." Later never came. Book sits there for weeks, accusing me. I won't start a new one because I haven't finished the old one. Habit dies. Year ends with three books read.

The unlock was a small permission I gave myself in early 2025. If a book hasn't grabbed me by page fifty, I'm allowed to stop. No guilt. No bookmark. No "I'll come back later." Close it, return it to the shelf, pick up the next one.

First time I tried it, with a celebrated literary novel everyone on Twitter was raving about, I felt physically lighter when I closed it. Second time, with a non-fiction book that was three chapters of one good idea stretched into ten of repetition, I felt cheated for the time I'd already spent. Third time, with a thriller that just wasn't for me, I felt nothing. Permission had become a habit.

I finished sixteen books that year. Year before, I'd finished four.

The math is obvious in retrospect. Stop the ones that aren't working and you've got time and attention left for the ones that are. The working ones pull you through. They make you want to keep reading. They make you say things at dinner like "you have to read this" or "I have to finish this chapter before I sleep."

The other thing that helped: keeping two books going at once. A heavier one (something technical or literary that needs focus), and a lighter one (fiction, memoir, whatever). The lighter one's what I reach for when I'm tired and would otherwise grab my phone. The heavier one is the morning coffee book. Having both means I'm never in "I'm too tired to read, so I'm not reading at all."

The bookshelf section on this site is a real-time receipt of this experiment. The ones marked current are what I'm actually working through. The ones not marked current are finished, with a one-line take. If a book's missing from the shelf, I started it and stopped at page fifty, and I don't feel a need to memorialize that. The permission, once given, also includes permission to not write about every book I tried.

If you're stuck in the moral-obligation phase of reading, the only advice I've got: quit one book this week. Quit one this month. Watch what happens to the rest of your year.
